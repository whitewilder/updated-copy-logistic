### **Meeting Notes**  

**1. Opening by Sarang Patel**  
- Sarang Patel initiated the meeting by discussing the minutes of the previous meeting and action items.  
- Highlighted the need to finalize definitions and classifications to avoid ambiguity in model applications.  

**2. Presentation by David Kang**  
- **Key Topics Addressed:**  
  a. Accurate definition and identification of Non-Financial Corporates.  
  b. The scope of corporate landscape models, including TLCS and mid-market models.  
  c. Differentiating between corporate asset classes, state-owned utilities, and public-sector entities.  

- **Key Clarifications by David Kang:**  
  - Entities completely funded by the government with no income fall outside the corporate scope.  
  - Subsidized entities operating commercially with significant revenue remain within scope.  
  - Clear distinction between corporates for regulatory definition and model-specific applications.  

**3. Discussion Points:**  
- **Patrick Odenwalder:**  
  - Sought clarification on ambiguous definitions and how asset classes are mapped.  
  - Highlighted potential exclusions like charities and purely government-funded entities.  
  - Questioned applicability to state-owned utilities and public transport organizations.  

- **David Kang:**  
  - Confirmed public-sector entities are excluded, but government-subsidized commercial entities may be considered.  
  - Stressed the need to ensure clarity on asset class categorization in documentation.  

- **Paul H Song:**  
  - Asked about the classification of private universities and colleges versus public institutions.  
  - David Kang confirmed private universities are corporates, while public ones depend on the nature of their operations.  

- **Chris Ellis:**  
  - Suggested incorporating definitions from regulatory frameworks like FINREP or classifications like NACE codes.  
  - Proposed exploring policy interpretations and consulting wider regulatory stakeholders.  

- **Markus Knubel:**  
  - Raised the importance of aligning GRA governance with broader organizational consensus.  
  - Suggested formalizing definitions and procedures for submission to regulators (e.g., PRA, ECB, HKMA).
